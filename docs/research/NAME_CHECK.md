# Preliminary name check: "Brumel"

> **This is not legal advice and not trademark clearance.** It is a superficial,
> preliminary search made on **2026-11-26** to spot obvious conflicts early. A search that
> finds nothing does **not** mean the name is free to use or register.

## Why "Brumel"

*Bruma* is Spanish for mist or haze. "Brumel" is short, easy to say in English and Spanish,
and evokes the product's identity: lightness, quiet and less noise. As a common noun it
does not describe browsers, which helps distinctiveness.

## Assessment

- **Software and browsers.** No conflicting browser or software product found in this
  superficial search.
- **Similar marks.** "Brumell" and "Brium" are close in spelling or sound and appear in
  software or business contexts. Whether they create a likelihood of confusion is a legal
  question for a professional.
- **Personal names.** Brumel is a surname (composer, athlete). This is generally not a barrier
  to a software name, but it affects searchability: "Brumel" alone returns mostly the composer.
  "Brumel Browser" is the better public name.
- **Game.** A Steam game uses "Brumel" in its title. That is a different market (video games),
  but class 9 (software) can overlap, so it should be reviewed.

**Working conclusion:** "Brumel" remains the working name. It must not be treated as cleared
until the steps below are complete.

## Limitations

- No search was made in official trademark registers. Web search engines do not index them
  reliably.
- Search results are regional (US-weighted) and change over time.
- Unregistered (common-law) rights and pending applications may not be visible.
- No search was made for phonetic variants in other languages (for example *Brumelle*,
  *Brummel*, *Brummell*). Note that "Beau Brummell" is a well-known historical figure and a
  name used by some brands.
